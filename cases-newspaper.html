Philosophy · Interactive fiction

The Choice

Can a game make moral philosophy personal? One branching story refuses to let the player settle for an easy answer.

A Build to Understand Studio project

Goal

To create an interactive game that offers a first encounter with moral dilemmas and the philosophical approaches used to navigate them.

How it was made

An interactive novel with an extensive branching dialogue system shaped by the player’s decisions. The game continually challenges the player’s position, showing that no single approach offers the only correct answer. At the end, it reveals which school of thought the player is closest to and suggests ideas to explore further.

Who uses it and how

Several universities use the game in Philosophy and Ethics courses—as an icebreaker, a first encounter with moral dilemmas and a starting point for classroom discussion.

Neuroscience · Quiz

How the Brain Shapes Behavior

A two-stage challenge turns an abstract model of the brain into a series of everyday decisions.

From the science desk

Goal

To spark curiosity about the triune brain model and let readers experience how it works in practice through a game.

How it was made

A two-stage multiple-choice quiz. Players first learn what each system is responsible for, then work through real-life scenarios and identify which system is in control.

Who uses it and how

A digital media publication used the quiz to draw readers into an article and help them assess their understanding at the end.

Justice · Artificial intelligence

Reasonable Doubt

When an algorithm offers a verdict, will you agree—and will everyone else?

From the technology desk

Goal

To introduce readers to the use of AI in the justice system and serve as the first step in a digital media content funnel.

How it was made

Players decide whether a person is guilty or not guilty using the evidence and an AI recommendation. After every decision, they see how others voted and whether they joined the majority.

Who uses it and how

Digital media publications can use the game as an engaging route into reporting on how AI supports decisions in courts and the wider justice system.

History & ideas

World history · Personal project

Evolution of Civilizations

Five societies develop in parallel as geography, resources and circumstance alter their paths.

Inspired by Jared Diamond’s Guns, Germs, and Steel

Goal

To introduce readers to the development of civilizations through the ideas presented in Jared Diamond’s book.

How it was made

Each turn introduces one factor explored in the book, showing how it affects five civilizations and why their outcomes differ.

Who uses it and how

A personal project made for the pleasure of turning a much-loved book into a game—and to help more people encounter its ideas.
